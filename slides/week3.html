The standard error falls to a quarter; the bias is unchanged

Block A · two kinds of data

Who chose the treatment decides what a comparison can show

Observational

Someone else decided who got what: managers, customers, history. The groups may differ in many ways besides the treatment.

Experimental

A random draw decided. The groups differ in the treatment, and otherwise only by chance.

Ask of every comparison: how were the groups formed?

Most business data is observational: logs, sales, records. That does not make it useless; it makes the question "who chose?" essential.

Block B · what helps

Compare within levels of the confounder, then combine with the same weights

  • Stratify: compare within rolls and within cakes
  • Standardise: apply each line's rates to the same mix of products
  • Both need the confounder to be known and recorded

The limit

An unrecorded confounder cannot be adjusted for. Only a random assignment protects against those.

Standardising the packing lines to a 50/50 mix: P (3% + 10%) / 2 = 6.5%, Q (4% + 13%) / 2 = 8.5%. The lab's stretch standardises the display comparison to the chain's mix of branch sizes.

Checkpoint

Which hospital would you choose?

Hospital H has a higher overall death rate than hospital K, but a lower rate both for routine and for emergency admissions. H takes most of the region's emergencies. Which hospital has the better record for a given kind of admission?

K, because its overall rate is lower
Neither: the figures contradict each other
H, because it does better within each kind of admission
Neither until both treat the same number of patients
Why. Admission type confounds the overall rate; within each type H does better.

Answer C. Remind the room that "better record" is still not proof of better care: other unrecorded differences could remain.

Block C · randomisation

A random assignment makes every difference between groups a matter of chance

  • Recorded or not, every characteristic is shared out by the same draw
  • The groups are not identical: by chance one may hold more large branches
  • So the difference in outcomes still varies: is it larger than chance alone would give? Week 6 answers that with a permutation test
Check balance: count the obvious characteristics in each arm, and report any imbalance rather than "fixing" it afterwards.

A common error: rerunning the draw until the groups look balanced and not saying so. Planned stratified or blocked assignment is the honest way to force balance; it is in SPC-EXP-201.

Block C · limits

An experiment answers one question about the units it was run on

Scope

24 branches in one city in one season: say so.

Compliance

A branch assigned the discount may not apply it every evening.

Size

Few units mean a lot of chance variation in the difference.

These three go into the limits line of the lab note. Non-compliance and intention-to-treat analysis are taught in SPC-EXP-201.

Week 3 · wrap-up

Three things to keep

Ask who chose

Observational groups can differ in everything.

Compare within

A recorded confounder can reverse an overall comparison.

Randomise when you can

It handles the confounders nobody recorded.

What does the 95% describe?

An analyst builds a 95% percentile bootstrap interval for the median waiting time from one sample. Which statement about it is correct?

95% of waits fall inside it
Its method contains the true median in about 95% of samples, if the method's coverage is right
The true median has a 95% chance of being inside this interval
95% of the bootstrap resamples contain the true median
Why. The level describes how often the method works, and only as far as its coverage holds at this n.

Block A · reporting rule

Suppress small groups before any table leaves your notebook

  • The course's rule: no group of fewer than 30 rows is reported
  • Blank the value and the exact count: "under 30"
  • A small group's mean is imprecise, and can point to identifiable people
cells = df.groupby(["plan", "health"])["visits"].agg(
    n="size", mean="mean")
shown = U.suppress(cells, "n", min_n=30)

The threshold of 30 is this course's own rule, not a national standard. Organisations set their own; follow theirs at work. The lab's table of visits by plan and health has small cells that must disappear.

Block B · differences

For a difference between two groups, resample each group separately

d = [rng.choice(a, len(a)).mean()
     - rng.choice(b, len(b)).mean()
     for _ in range(B)]
low, high = np.percentile(d, [2.5, 97.5])
  • Each group keeps its own size, as it was collected
  • Pooling the groups would resample "no difference": that is next week's test
  • Any statistic: difference of means, of medians, a ratio

Make the contrast explicit now, because week 6 shuffles the pooled values: the bootstrap estimates how big the difference is; the permutation test asks whether chance alone could make it.

Block B · a misreading

Compare groups with an interval for their difference, not by overlapping intervals

  • Two 95% intervals can overlap while the interval for the difference excludes zero
  • The rule "no overlap means a real difference" is too strict; "overlap means none" is wrong
  • The question is about the difference: estimate the difference

This is one of the ten-statement misreadings of LO2. The lab builds the difference's interval directly.

Block C · slopes

For a slope, resample whole rows so each x keeps its own y

Five points: (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 5), (4, 4), (5, 7)

Least-squares slope: 1.0

Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) / Σ(x − x̄)² = 10 / 10

i = rng.integers(0, n, n)     # rows
b = slope(x[i], y[i])
What goes wrong. Resampling x and y separately breaks the pairs: every slope comes out near zero.

From slide_numbers.py, week 5. FND-MTH-101 derived least squares as a projection; here the slope is used, not derived. Residual and wild bootstraps exist; they are beyond this course.

Block C · reruns

An interval you report must survive a rerun with a new seed

  • Run every interval twice, with two seeds
  • Measure the largest move of an end against the interval's width
  • Report to the precision that does not move

The outcome's evidence

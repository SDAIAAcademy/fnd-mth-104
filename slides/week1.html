The case study

All term you are the new analyst of a chain of 24 neighbourhood bakeries

  • Branches BR01 to BR24 in five sectors of one city; product lines L01 to L20
  • Four readers ask the questions: the operations manager, the quality lead, the finance analyst and the customer insight lead
  • Every file is synthetic and generated with a fixed seed, so everyone gets the same numbers

Why synthetic

You can know the truth. In weeks 2, 4 and 8 a whole quarter of baskets plays the population, so every estimate can be checked against it.

The chain, its city and its people have no names on purpose: roles and codes only. Weeks 5 and 6 add two public-domain datasets so that participants also practise on real data.

Block A · robustness

One mistyped value moves the mean and leaves the median alone

92.78 → 492.78
the mean, when 400 is keyed as 4000
55.00 → 55.00
the median, unchanged
Robust means one bad row cannot drag the summary far. The median and the trimmed mean are robust; the mean and the standard deviation are not.

From slide_numbers.py, week 1. The mean moves by the error divided by n: 3,600 / 9 = 400. Robustness is not a virtue in itself: the mean is the right summary for totals, and then the error must be found and fixed, not hidden.

Block A · shape

Money, waits and counts usually have a long right tail

  • Most values are small; a few are much larger; none are below zero
  • The mean sits above the median, pulled by the tail
  • Skewness measures the lean: 0 for a symmetric shape, large and positive for a long right tail
  • On a log scale the tail often becomes nearly symmetric
For a report. Say which summary you used and why: "median basket" and "mean basket" are different numbers.

Ask the room for other long-tailed quantities from their own work: invoice values, response times, file sizes. The lab measures the skewness of the baskets before and after taking logarithms.

Checkpoint

Which summary for this request?

The finance analyst must forecast next month's total delivery charges from this month's 2,000 deliveries, whose charges have a long right tail. Which summary of this month's charges does the forecast need?

The median charge
The 20% trimmed mean charge
The mean charge
The interquartile range of the charges
Why. A total is the mean times the count; the tail contributes to the total, so the mean is the summary that adds up.

Answer C. If many pick the median, return to slide "mean and median answer different questions": the question decides.

Block B · spread

The nine ordersValue

IQR = Q3 − Q110.00

  • The standard deviation is dominated by the one large order
  • The IQR describes the eight ordinary ones
  • Percentiles have several rules; NumPy's default interpolates. Say which you used

From slide_numbers.py, week 1. NumPy's default places the p-th percentile of n sorted values at position (n − 1)p counting from zero: for nine values Q1 is the third value, 50, and Q3 the seventh, 60. Other software may give slightly different quartiles on small samples.

Block B · evidence

Decide "error or real" from evidence in the other columns

Value per item

Ten items for 400 is plausible; three items for 4,000 is not.

Source

A till known to repeat a key, a manual entry, an import from another system.

Context

A catering booking that day; a branch that was closed; a time outside opening hours.

Rule of the course: remove only what you can show is an error, remove it alone, and say so in the note.

The lab has one of each: an error and a real order. Do not name them here; the lab asks participants to classify the two largest baskets themselves.

Checkpoint

What do you do with this order?

A month of 3,000 coffee orders has a high fence of 31 riyals. Forty orders lie above it, the largest 96 riyals for 14 drinks at a branch next to an office block. What do you do?

Keep it: value per drink is ordinary, so it is a real large order
Delete all forty orders above the fence
Replace it with the fence value, 31
Delete it, because it will distort the median
Why. 96 / 14 is under 7 riyals a drink: nothing suggests an error, so it stays.

Answer A. Ask what evidence would change the answer: three drinks for 96 riyals, or a branch closed that day.

Block C · missing data

Missing values have a cause, and the cause decides the bias

Why the value is missingWhat dropping the rows does
By chance: a form lost at randomFewer rows, no bias
Linked to something recorded: more gaps on weekendsBias, fixable by comparing within the recorded groups
Linked to the value itself: long waits not logged when staff are busyBias that the data cannot reveal: waits look shorter than they are

The textbook names are missing completely at random, at random, and not at random; the plain-language version is enough here. Ask: in a satisfaction survey, who is most likely not to answer?

Block C · codes

A missing value may not look missing: check for codes before you summarise

  • Empty cells become NaN when read
  • Some systems write 0, −1, 999 or a date like 1900-01-01 instead
  • A code left as a number quietly enters the mean
df["x"].isna().mean()          # share empty
(df["x"] < 0).sum()             # impossible values
df.loc[df["x"] < 0, "x"] = np.nan  # decode, then report

A negative wait is impossible, so it is a code. Ask what a zero wait could mean: a real instant service or an unrecorded one. The data dictionary, or the person who runs the system, decides.

Block C · reporting

Report the missing share and its likely direction beside every summary

A sentence that does the job has four parts

The summary of the recorded values; the share not recorded; where the gaps are; and which way they probably move the true value.

Every lab ends with a note: the result, how it was checked, its limits, and who reruns it before it is used. The analyst who ran it does not sign it off alone.

Introduce the four-part note here: participants write one at the end of every lab, and the capstone brief needs one for each result, including any AI-assisted step.

Checkpoint

Which way does the bias run?

A delivery service logs delivery times by hand. Drivers often skip the log when they are running late. The median of the logged times is 28 minutes. What is the most likely relation to the true median?

About the same, since a median ignores missing rows
The true median is lower than 28 minutes
Impossible to say, because medians are robust
The true median is higher than 28 minutes
Why. Late deliveries are the ones not logged, so the logged times are the shorter ones.

Answer D. The direction is known even though its size is not: say so in the note.

Debrief · lab 1.1

Waits were missing in 21% of the busiest branch's evenings, about 2% elsewhere

  • Overall only 3.6% of waits are missing: the average hides where they are
  • The busiest branch's median recorded wait (4.1 minutes) is already the longest, and probably understated
  • One branch's till wrote −1 for "not recorded": decoded before any summary

From the solution run of lab 1.1. Filling the gaps with the overall median or with the branch-and-band median moves the mean wait only in the second decimal (stretch): the honest report is the missing share and its direction, not a filled number.

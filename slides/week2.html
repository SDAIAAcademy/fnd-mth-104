Which change moves the median?

Eleven parcel weights have a median of 2.4 kg. Which single change can move the median?

Doubling the heaviest parcel
Halving the lightest parcel
Adding two more parcels, both heavier than 2.4 kg
Rounding every weight to one decimal
Why. Two values above the middle shift which parcel is in the middle: the median moves up or stays, depending on the next value.

Answer C. Rounding to one decimal cannot move a median already given to one decimal. Use the answer to recall robustness: the median moves only when the middle of the order changes.

The sampling distribution

The list of every possible value of the statistic, with how often each occurs. Here: 6.0, 7.5 and 10.5, each one time in three. Their standard deviation is 1.87.

Which number is a statistic?

An auditor checks 80 of a branch's 1,200 invoices for the month and finds 6 with errors. Which of these is a statistic?

The share of errors among the 80 checked, 7.5%
The 1,200 invoices of the month
The share of errors among all 1,200 invoices
The number of invoices checked, 80
Why. A statistic is computed from the sample; 6 / 80 = 7.5% would differ for another 80 invoices.

Answer A.

Block B · standard error

The standard error is the spread of a statistic from sample to sample

  • Not the spread of the values: the spread of the statistic
  • It answers: how far does my estimate typically land from the truth?
  • By simulation: the standard deviation of the thousands of statistics

Two spreads, two questions

Standard deviation of baskets: how different are customers? Standard error of the mean basket: how sure is my estimate?

The most common confusion of the term. Ask a participant to explain the card to a neighbour in their own words.

Block B · the formula

For a mean, the standard error falls like one over the square root of n

σ = 12nσ / √n

1441.00
5760.50
  • Four times the data halves the standard error
  • σ is unknown in practice: s / √n from one sample stands in
  • For a median there is no simple formula: simulate

From slide_numbers.py, week 2. Precision is expensive: each halving costs four times the sample. The lab compares the simulated standard error with σ/√n and with s/√n from one sample.

Block B · recap from FND-MTH-103

Sample means tend to a normal shape as n grows, slowly on a long tail

  • The central limit theorem: for large n the sampling distribution of a mean is close to normal
  • "Large" depends on the population: the longer its tail, the larger n must be
  • Simulation shows the actual shape at your n; you do not need to assume it
Pointer. FND-MTH-103 covered the theorem and where the normal approximation fails. Here it is one reason to prefer simulation.

One slide only: this is FND-MTH-103's topic. The lab measures the skewness of the sampling distribution at n = 10 and n = 400 on the baskets.

Block C · the frame

The sampling frame decides which population a sample can speak for

  • The frame is the list you can actually draw from: members with contact details, a phone directory, today's visitors
  • A random sample from the frame represents the frame, not those outside it
  • If those outside differ, the estimate is biased

Example

A survey of daytime callers misses everyone who shops after work; their baskets may differ.

Ask the room for the frame of a survey they know and who it misses. The lab measures, against a known truth, how far a members-only frame is from all customers.

Block C · two errors

Sample size fixes imprecision; it does not fix bias

Imprecision

Estimates scatter around the truth. Shrinks with n, like 1/√n. Measured by the standard error.

Bias

Estimates centre on the wrong value. The same at every n. Not shown by the standard error at all.

What goes wrong. A huge sample from the wrong frame gives a narrow interval around the wrong number.

Draw two targets on the board: shots scattered around the centre, and shots tightly grouped away from it. The second is worse and looks better.

Checkpoint

Precise, but about what?

A gym estimates members' weekly visits from 5,000 check-ins logged by its entrance machine, which records only members who use the app to enter. What is the main weakness?

The sample is too small for a stable mean
Visit counts have a large standard deviation
Visits are whole numbers, so the mean is rounded
The frame leaves out members who enter another way
Why. The estimate is precise about app users only; if others visit more or less, it is biased whatever the size.

Answer D. Ask what frame would fix it: every entry, however the member came in.

Week 2 · wrap-up

Three things to keep

Statistics vary

A new sample, a new answer; the sampling distribution shows how much.

SE is that variation

The spread of the statistic; for a mean it falls like 1/√n.

Frames decide

Size buys precision, never representativeness.
